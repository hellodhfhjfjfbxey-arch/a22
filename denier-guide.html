Denier Explained

Denier is the number on every packet of tights. Here is what it means, and which to choose for every occasion.

iWhat denier means

Denier measures the thickness of the yarn: the weight in grams of 9,000 metres of a single thread. The higher the number, the thicker the yarn, the more opaque the tights and usually the warmer and stronger they are.

iiThe scale at a glance

  • 5 to 10: ultra-sheer, almost invisible.
  • 15 to 20: sheer, the classic for dresses and evenings.
  • 30 to 40: semi-opaque, everyday and versatile.
  • 60 to 100: opaque, warm and hard-wearing.
  • Over 100: thick, almost like leggings, for winter.

iiiChoosing for the occasion

Sheer tights suit formal and evening wear and lighter fabrics. Semi-opaque works for office days. Opaque tights look modern with boots, knitwear and wool skirts, and keep you warm when the temperature drops.

ivColour and denier together

Sheer black looks darker at the edges of the leg and lighter at the front, which slims and shapes. Opaque colours read as solid blocks; match them to your shoes for a long line, or contrast them for a bold look.

vDurability

Lower denier tights are more delicate and ladder more easily. Many brands add reinforced toes, a stronger panty section or a little extra elastane to help sheer pairs last longer.
